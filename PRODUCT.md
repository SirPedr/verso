# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three readers Verso writes for:

- **Spotify referrals** — followed the link from the artist profile, here to read the lyrics behind the songs.
- **Returning readers** — bookmarked Verso, come back for new posts. They already know the voice.
- **First-timers** — landed via a share, no context. Need a quick read on what this is before they invest.

All read in a private, low-light context: late at night, on a phone, in a quiet moment. The content is melancholic and introspective in Portuguese (pt-BR). Readers are not browsing, they are settling in. The primary act is **reading a post, comfortably, slowly**. Everything else (navigation, About, framing copy) serves that one act.

## Product Purpose

Verso is a personal blog for creative writing: song lyrics, poetry, and prose, with recurring themes of solitude, existential questioning, love and loss, and the search for meaning. The author writes the words and produces the music (catalog on Spotify). The site exists to give those words a home that reads the way the songs sound. Success is a reader who slows down, finishes the piece, and comes back.

## Brand Personality

**Three words: voiced, melancholic, printerly.**

The 404 line sets the tone: *"Perdido nos versos."* Wordplay, intimate, a little sad, gently literary. Every framing copy moment should sound like that line wrote it, written, not generated. The aesthetic is old-print sensibility rendered on a screen at night: a printed page held under a single lamp, not a glowing dashboard. Restraint over density, generous negative space, stillness over motion. The I-beam mark is the project's typographic signature, a glyph that can recur (between posts, as a separator, as the footer ornament in place of ©).

## Anti-references

- SaaS-onboarding cheer.
- Medium polish.
- Generic "literary blog" templates (serif-on-dark boilerplate).
- Any "tech" energy: accent glow, gradients, cyan-on-black, infinite-feed pull.

## Design Principles

1. **Voice everywhere, or nowhere.** The 404 line is the standard, not the exception. Framing copy sounds written, in the author's Portuguese voice.
2. **Dark as paper, not as glow.** Light text on dark reads as ink on coated paper at night. No accent glow, no gradients.
3. **Distinctive type or no type.** The display serif carries the voice and is the most visible design decision. Generic supporting sans (the current Inter is a placeholder) is to be replaced, not leaned on. Mono is reserved for metadata only.
4. **The reader is settled in, not skimming.** Optimize for slow reading: long line-heights, ample margin around posts, no infinite-feed energy. Prose and verse are differentiated (drop cap and hanging punctuation for essays; tighter, narrower measure for lyrics).
5. **One mark, recurring.** The I-beam is a print glyph, not a logo. Use it as a section separator, a footer ornament, an end-of-text mark.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Target: WCAG AA.** Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 against the warm-sepia dark ground.
- Visible focus ring on every interactive element.
- Reduced-motion fallback for the page-enter transition (already honored).
- `lang="pt-BR"` set correctly so hyphenation and screen readers pronounce the content properly.
- Visited links styled deliberately (no default browser purple against the dark palette).
